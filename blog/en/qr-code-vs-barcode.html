QR Code vs Barcode: What Is the Difference?

2026-10-15 · 3 min read

Both codes turn a printed pattern into digital data, so people often use the two words interchangeably. They are built on very different ideas, though, and choosing the wrong one makes a job harder than it needs to be. Here is how a QR code and a traditional barcode actually compare.

One-Dimensional vs Two-Dimensional

A barcode is one-dimensional: it stores data in the widths and spacing of parallel bars read along a single line. A QR code is two-dimensional: it stores data across a grid of black and white modules, both horizontally and vertically. That extra dimension is why a QR code can hold so much more.

Capacity and Error Correction

The practical differences come down to two numbers:

  • Capacity — a barcode holds a few dozen characters, enough for a product number; a QR code holds hundreds to thousands, enough for a full URL or a vCard;
  • Error correction — a barcode has little to none, so a scratch can break it; a QR code can lose up to about 30% of its surface and still read.

Scan Direction

A barcode must be swept across the reader in the right orientation, which is why supermarket scanners feel so tactile. A QR code can be read from any direction because its corner finder patterns tell the reader where the code is and how it is rotated. That flexibility is why phones can scan one at an angle without any aiming skill.

When to Use Each

Barcodes remain ideal for retail checkout, inventory labels and anywhere a scanner is fixed and a short number is all that is needed.
